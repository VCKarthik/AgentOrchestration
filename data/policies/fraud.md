# Fraud Investigation Policy

## Purpose
Define the protocols for detecting, investigating, and responding to suspected fraud cases.

## Fraud Classification

| Level | Description | Response Time | Escalation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Level 1 — Suspected | Anomaly flagged by system | 5 business days | Team lead |
| Level 2 — Probable | Multiple corroborating indicators | 48 hours | Compliance Officer |
| Level 3 — Confirmed | Evidence of deliberate misrepresentation | 24 hours | General Counsel + CFO |
| Level 4 — Criminal | Evidence of criminal conduct | Immediate | Law enforcement + Board |

## Fraud Indicators
Any case exhibiting 3 or more of the following must be escalated to Level 2:
- Duplicate submissions (same claim, multiple requestors)
- Altered or forged documentation
- Unusual payment routing or beneficiary changes
- Transactions just below approval thresholds ("structuring")
- Rapid repeated transactions with the same counterparty
- Requestor refuses to provide supporting documentation
- IP address or device mismatch with known user profile

## Mandatory Actions — Confirmed Fraud
1. Freeze associated accounts or payment channels immediately
2. Preserve all digital evidence (do not delete or modify records)
3. Notify Compliance Officer within 1 hour of confirmation
4. File Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) if financial threshold exceeded ($5,000+)
5. No internal notification to suspect until legal counsel advises

## Investigation Protocols
- All investigations must be assigned a unique Case ID with complete audit trail
- Investigator must have no prior relationship with the subject party
- External forensics required for Level 3+ cases
- Investigation notes are privileged — legal hold must be placed on all records

## Compliance
All fraud investigations must comply with applicable AML regulations, FinCEN guidance, FCPA, and the organization's Code of Conduct. Retaliation against fraud reporters is strictly prohibited.
